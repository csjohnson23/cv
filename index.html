Research #

AI for Cancer Precision Medicine

In precision oncology, we usually study the tumor in isolation: which tumors or cell lines respond to a treatment, and which don't. Machine learning models for these questions are typically built on tumor data alone. But when we treat a patient, we treat the whole person, not a tumor in a vacuum. A patient's germline genetics and systemic traits plausibly shape which tumor develops, how it evolves, and how well a treatment works.

My research puts the tumor back in the context of the host, modeling the two together to improve cancer models and clinical decisions. I approach this across a spectrum of resolution. At the finest level, I study gene-level convergence between a patient's germline and their tumor: which genes are involved and how they interact. Moving outward, I develop ways to bring the whole germline genome into machine learning models without losing signal we can't specify in advance, and I use polygenic scores and GWAS to bring population genetics into host-tumor models. At the broadest level, I build multimodal representation learning models that integrate germline and somatic genomics into a first-of-its-kind joint representation space, study its structure for new host-tumor relationships, and use its embeddings to improve downstream clinical predictions. Agentic systems then carry this host context into treatment recommendations for individual patients.

Per Dana-Farber policy, the methods, evals, results, and titles of this work are proprietary and cannot be shared ahead of journal release. The projects below give the general topic and a description of each, from most to least recent.

Research projects

  1. Multi-agent framework for reasoning across data modalities in cancer treatment decision support

    In preparation

    A cancer patient's first treatment is their best chance. The right drug might send a patient into remission, while the wrong one not only delays effective treatment and significantly reduces patient health, but often acts as a mutagen on the tumor, making further treatments less likely to succeed. Predicting which patients will respond to a treatment before it is prescribed is therefore inestimably important, and remains a largely open question for most treatments.

    This project built a multi-agent AI system that reasons over different data modalities for one patient, bringing systemic host context into treatment recommendations alongside embeddings of tumor somatic information, clinical notes, lab values, and both external and internal knowledge bases. The result is a contextualized treatment recommendation grounded in patient data. The system was trained on more than 60,000 patients from the DFCI PROFILE cohort, TCGA, and the Hartwig Medical Foundation.

    Method details, evaluation metrics, and results are proprietary.

  2. Multimodal representation learning for germline and somatic genomics reveals novel host-tumor associations

    Submitted

    Most models of cancer genomics focus on the tumor itself: somatic mutations, single-cell RNA-seq, H&E images, and spatial biology sampled from the tumor. But cancer grows inside a host, and when we treat cancer, we treat the whole host, not the tumor in isolation. Host-level attributes may therefore shape tumor evolution, the somatic mutational profile, and even treatment outcomes.

    In this project, we integrated tumor somatic and host germline genomics into one representation learning model, to interrogate the shared host-tumor latent space for novel germline-somatic associations and to assess the prognostic value of the joint latent space for downstream clinical endpoints.

    Method details, evaluation metrics, and results are proprietary.

  3. Novel approach to high clinical signal dimensionality reduction produces germline genomic representations associated with overall survival, comorbidities, and adverse events in cancer patients

    Submitted

    The human genome contains more than a billion catalogued genetic variants. The largest germline sequencing datasets, such as the UK Biobank, include about 500,000 genomes, and cancer cohorts with germline data number fewer than 100,000 patients: fewer than one patient for every 10,000 variants. To bring the germline into oncology modeling tasks, we need dimensionality reduction methods that retain the clinically important signal in a patient's germline beyond genetic ancestry, while greatly reducing the number of features in the germline vector.

    We introduce a novel germline genome representation that uses publicly available data to prioritize likely high-signal variants, then weights those features with pre-trained effect sizes from off-trait GWAS and polygenic scores. We then assess the clinical utility of the representation through its associations with adverse events, comorbidities, and survival in cancer patients.

    Method details, evaluation metrics, and results are proprietary.

  4. Signal distillation for improved cross-cohort transfer of polygenic scores

    Submitted

    Polygenic scores are almost always evaluated against healthy or population controls. We benchmarked hundreds of PGS Catalog cancer scores on a harder and more clinically relevant task: distinguishing patients with one cancer from patients with other cancers, with no healthy controls. We scored more than 30,000 patients across cohorts with very different germline data sources, including genotypes imputed from off-target reads of tumor panel sequencing (DFCI PROFILE), blood and tissue-normal genotypes (TCGA), and whole genomes (Hartwig Medical Foundation).

    We found that a cancer's related scores are imperfectly redundant views of one shared genetic axis. Differences in the single best score per cohort are driven by measurement noise and variant matching, and importantly, the score trained on the largest or most recent GWAS is not always among the best performers. We present a novel signal distillation method, and show that the distilled score sits closer to the shared cross-cohort signal than any individual score: built in one cohort and applied unchanged to another, it transfers more cleanly than the best single score.

    Method details, evaluation metrics, and results are proprietary.

Technical Advising #

Productionizing AI: ML-Ops, LLM-Ops, and AI-Ops #

Teaching Fellow and Technical Advisor, APCOMP 215, Harvard SEAS · Fall 2025

In this course, teams of three to five students designed, built, and deployed their own AI products in a single term: working web or mobile applications with a meaningful modeling component, comparable in scope to a small production-ready app. Each project covered the full lifecycle of an AI system, from data pipelines and model training or fine-tuning to LLM integration, agentic workflows, containerized cloud deployment, and a complete user-facing interface.

I shepherded each of my teams' projects from inception through scalable deployment, working hands-on with students through every phase: scoping the idea, designing the architecture, implementing it, and deploying it.

Advised student projects

  1. PredictBG

    An iOS app that forecasts blood glucose up to 30 minutes ahead using Transformer-based time-series models trained on Type 1 diabetes CGM datasets. It also estimates carbohydrates from meal photos and uses a RAG advisor grounded in clinical guidelines to turn forecasts into practical advice.

  2. FirstAid LLM

    A retrieval-augmented first-aid assistant that answers questions with citations drawn from 99+ authoritative medical guidelines. A DistilBERT classifier flags life-threatening emergencies and prompts users to call 911, and voice input allows hands-free questions.

    Live demoBlog post

  3. Duped

    A skincare dupe finder that recommends cheaper, functionally similar products. Products are embedded from their ingredient lists and searched in a Qdrant vector index, an agent enriches results with live shopping data, and a dedicated service flags risky ingredient interactions. Users can search by product name, ingredient list, or a photo of the label.

  4. Evea

    An iOS app that connects menstrual cycle logs and symptoms with wearable data from HealthKit to give phase-aware, research-grounded recommendations for managing PMS. User events stream through Pub/Sub into Cloud Run and Cloud SQL, and a RAG service on Vertex AI generates each day's insights.

  5. Lyv Health

    Lyv Intelligence, an AI layer for Lyv Health's women's longevity and preventive medicine platform. The team built containerized microservices on GCP in which an ML API turns each member's health data into a personalized prompt, grounded by a RAG pipeline over MongoDB and developed against generated synthetic user data. Lyv Health has since rebranded as Altro Health and operates as a startup.

    Demo videoAltro Health

  6. DoseWise

    A counterfactual prediction system that lets clinicians see how a drug is likely to affect a patient's vital signs before it's given, comparing predicted vitals with and without treatment and adding LLM-generated clinical summaries. Built on a VitalDB-to-BigQuery training pipeline with a FastAPI inference service and a Next.js dashboard.

    Blog post

Evolved Technology #

Technical Mentor and Judge · Fall 2025 and Spring 2026

Through Evolved's AI x Bio hackathon, I advised startup and research teams on their technical approach and implementation. The specifics of that work are proprietary, so the teams are listed below with the general focus of each project.

Advised teams

  1. Unlock Biology

    Built an agent-in-the-loop extension for Nextflow that reviews QC metrics and artifacts mid-pipeline, proposes parameter re-sweeps, and re-runs only the affected branches, logging each decision to a reusable graph of past analyses.

  2. Raycaster

    Developed an agentic CMC and regulatory copilot that turns batch records, specifications, and stability data into source-cited drafts of Module 3 submission content.

  3. Biolligence

    Developed a multi-agent drug safety suite that extracts toxicity signals from chemical and omics data, maps them to biological pathways, and checks them against public safety databases such as FAERS and ChEMBL.

  4. GenoBank.io

    Built an air-gapped MCP server that lets AI agents analyze genomic data locally, with no external API calls, while tracking data ownership and provenance on-chain.

  5. M3TRIQ

    Combined protein and molecular foundation models with multi-agent orchestration to design protein–ligand interactions that can replace growth factors in serum-free cell culture.

  6. GenoCache Align

    Developed a GPU-accelerated genomic aligner that replaces traditional seeding with fine-tuned DNA transformer embeddings and approximate nearest-neighbor search over cached reference genome tiles.

  7. Kepler AI

    Automated bioinformatics workflows by using language models to enrich and structure the unstructured metadata in large biological data sources.

  8. HelioSynth Research

    Developed a multi-agent decision support system that integrates imaging, genomic, and clinical data into explainable diagnostic and treatment reports for cancer care.

  9. Varosync

    Designed GPCR-targeted protein binders using enhanced molecular dynamics and AI-guided transition path sampling to capture cryptic binding pockets in their native membrane context.

  10. Fastfold AI

    Built a binder design loop that adds selectivity scoring against off-target proteins and LLM-based filtering for properties such as solubility, stability, and immunogenicity.

  11. Gardn Biosciences

    Applied Design by Integrated Gradients, a differentiability-free framework that turns model interpretability into generative design, to design complete mRNAs optimized for translation, stability, and structure.

  12. microDAO

    Fine-tuned a protein language model to generate novel antimicrobial peptides, paired with deep learning predictors of activity and toxicity in an iterative generate, score, and refine loop.

  13. Multi-D @ Elegant Mathematics

    Scaled an equivariant graph neural network foundation model that generates conformational ensembles and energies for small molecules, trained on up to 1.7 billion conformers.

  14. Leash Biosciences

    Trained a binding model on DNA-encoded library data covering more than 500 proteins and 6.5 million molecules, testing whether it responds to binding pocket mutations and can locate known pockets.

  15. Silico Biosciences

    Tuned language models on curated antibody–antigen complex sequences to generate antibodies with high binding affinity for target antigens, extending their peleke-1 models.

  16. Bhalesha

    Developed genomic AI models that infer an embryo's genome from cell-free DNA and parental whole genomes, enabling non-invasive screening for chromosomal and single-gene disorders.

  17. Bacformer (University of Cambridge)

    Pretrained a bacterial foundation model that represents whole organisms as ordered sequences of proteins along the genome, aimed at uncovering the genetic drivers of antibiotic resistance and other phenotypes.

  18. Virgo

    Extended EndoDINO, their foundation model for endoscopic images, toward video using a dataset of nearly 3 million endoscopy procedures, to explore opportunistic screening for kidney and liver disease.

  19. Diatom Biolab

    Trained a compositional model on large single-perturbation datasets to predict the effects of combined gene and drug perturbations, focusing on synthetic lethal interactions in DNA damage repair.

  20. Tomorrow Taste (formerly Weekend Inc.)

    Built a multimodal model that combines binding, reporter cell line, and sensory panel data to predict the taste properties of designed proteins.

  21. Allegoria

    Trained a DINOv3-based foundation model for dermatology that predicts 3D skin structure from ordinary 2D images, toward a non-invasive alternative to biopsy for skin cancer diagnosis.

  22. Markov Biosciences

    Trained cross-layer transcoders on their virtual cell model to explain the mechanism of action of the oncology drug ralimetinib at the level of interpretable features, and to find response biomarkers.

  23. Longevity Genie

    Fine-tuned the Cell2Sentence foundation model on age-annotated single-cell data, then ran virtual screens of genetic interventions to find those that reverse age-related cellular changes.
